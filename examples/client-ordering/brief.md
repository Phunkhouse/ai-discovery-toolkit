## Problem

- **Request:** an app and a portal for customers to order online instead of phoning or emailing (SRC-1).
- **Problem statement:** Office staff and customers lose time and accuracy when routine trade orders are taken by phone and email and re-keyed into the ERP, which leads to staff mornings spent on order entry, wrong deliveries and credit notes. It is solved when routine reorders reach the ERP without re-keying and wrong-item credit notes fall, without customers losing the option to call.
- **Opportunity:** about 2,100 orders a quarter (F-1), 93% by phone or email (F-2); wrong-item credit notes at about 1.5 per 100 orders (F-3). Office time on order entry is reported, not measured (S-1).
- **Affected:** customers (ordering effort, wrong items), office staff (order entry), warehouse (picking wrong items), bookkeeper (credit notes), sales reps (relationship and commission).
- **Today:** orders arrive by phone and email and are typed into the ERP; detail in W-1 and W-2.
- **Why now:** the MD wants office time back before the spring season and is open to spending on it (C-1).

## User workflows

- **W-1 Phone reorder, today:** customer — runs low → phones the office → staff search products by name, type the order into the ERP, read it back → warehouse picks next morning. Hurts at: similar product names (S-3), busy phone lines in the morning (S-1), customer does not know the delivery day (S-4).
- **W-2 Rep visit order, today:** rep — visits a customer → writes the order → office types it in later. Hurts at: delay and re-keying; 7% of orders (F-2).
- **W-3 Online reorder, pilot:** customer — runs low → logs in on a phone or computer → reopens a previous order, adjusts quantities, sees the delivery day for the current time → submits → order is in the ERP, credited to the account's rep; confirmation email. Failure cases: order after cutoff shows the next delivery day before submitting; an item out of stock is flagged, not silently dropped; a customer without a login, or who prefers to, can still phone. Replaces W-1 for routine reorders only.

| O-4 | Off-the-shelf B2B ordering service connected to the ERP. | M-H | M | M | L | Fallback if O-2 fails H-3; integration unknown (Q-6). |
| O-5 | Do nothing. | — | — | M | H | Errors and office time continue (F-3, S-1). |

**Recommendation (D-2, approved):** Validate first, then adopt O-2 for a pilot. The module already knows customers and prices (F-4), so the riskiest parts are whether customers switch (A-2) and whether it meets the must requirements (A-3), both cheap to test before committing. O-4 is the fallback if H-3 fails; O-1 is not justified until a pilot shows demand that O-2 cannot serve. The commission conflict (X-1) must be settled before the pilot or reps may sink it.

## Scope

- **Must have:** a pilot of the ERP ordering module with about 15 customers: own prices and reorder (R-1), delivery day before submit (R-2), orders straight into the ERP (R-3), rep credited (R-4), phone ordering unchanged (R-8).
- **Optional:** order confirmation email (R-5), then stock availability (R-6).
- **Non-goals:** a native app in the pilot (R-7); replacing the ERP (C-2); online payment; ordering for new customers who have no account.
- **Deferred:** rollout to all customers, until H-2 passes (D-3); reps' visit orders (W-2) on a tablet, after the pilot.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] **AC-1** A pilot customer reorders a previous order on a phone browser in under two minutes; checked by a walkthrough with three pilot customers (R-1).
- [ ] **AC-2** The order appears in the ERP with the customer's own prices and no re-keying; checked on ten pilot orders (R-1, R-3).
- [ ] **AC-3** An order started after the cutoff shows the next delivery day before submitting; checked by a test order after the cutoff (R-2).
- [ ] **AC-4** Online orders appear under the account's rep in the commission report; checked on the first month's report (R-4).
- [ ] **AC-5** A pilot customer can still phone an order in the usual way; checked by one phoned order during the pilot (R-8).

## Success

- **Outcome:** routine reorders reach the ERP without re-keying, wrong deliveries fall, and customers who want to call still can.
- **Could fail or backfire:** reps steer customers away (RK-1; watch M-1 by rep); customers order online and phone to check (count confirmation calls); pricing errors in the module reach customers (AC-2 sample, first two weeks).
