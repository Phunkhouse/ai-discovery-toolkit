# Walkthrough: tutor matching

An early-stage product with ambiguous requirements, a disagreement between founders and a request to put unsupported numbers in front of investors. All data is synthetic. Files: [input](input.md), [brief](brief.md).

## Session 1 (27 of 30 minutes)

**Framing.** The input described a product, two customer groups and a revenue model, but no problem. The agent recorded the product as option O-1 and framed the problem for the group the founder considered most urgent, explicitly as a hypothesis (A-1), because nothing yet showed that the problem exists.

**Evidence.** "Lots of parents love the idea" went in as a statement. One question turned it into something usable:

> Who exactly did you talk to, and what did they say about how they find a tutor today? It decides whether we build or test first.

The answer (about twelve informal conversations with friends, no notes) stayed a statement (S-1). Interest in a concept is not evidence of a problem, and the brief says so.

**Fabricated metrics.** The founder asked for "10,000 users in year one" and "AI matching improves learning outcomes by 30%" in the investor deck. The agent explained once that neither has evidence, then proposed D-5: show 10,000 users as a target and leave out the 30% claim. The founder agreed. Neither number appears anywhere in the brief as a fact or an estimate.

**Conflict.** Subscription versus commission became X-1. The agent pointed out that the test does not depend on it and proposed deferring it (D-4) rather than resolving it for the founders.

**Scope reduction.** Four options, from the full marketplace to interviews only. The agent recommended *validate first* with a concierge test and interviews: no code, one segment, one city, four weeks. AI matching, payments, video, reviews and adult learners all became non-goals or deferred, each with its reason. A regulatory unknown (checks for tutors working with minors) became Q-3 and blocks the first lesson with a minor, not the whole test.

**Partial approval.** The founder approved D-1, D-2, D-4 and D-5 but wanted the co-founder's view on the segment (D-3). The agent recorded exactly that: D-3 and the test scope (D-6) stay `proposed`, the status is `awaiting approval`, and *Resume here* names the next step.

## What this example shows

- **Ambiguous requirements:** two segments and a feature list reduced to one testable problem.
- **No build yet:** the recommendation is a test that needs an existing form tool, not software.
- **Human ownership:** partial approval is recorded as partial; nothing the co-founder has not seen is marked decided.
- **Honest numbers:** targets are labelled as targets, and an unsupported outcome claim is left out instead of softened into an "estimate".
