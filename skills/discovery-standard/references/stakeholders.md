# Stakeholders and asynchronous discovery

## Map the stakeholders

List every role with an interest in the outcome or a say in it, as `P-` rows:

- **Interest:** what they need from the outcome, and what they fear from it. Fears explain resistance better than needs do.
- **Influence:** how much they can shape or block the outcome (high, medium, low), with the reason when it is not obvious (budget, sign-off, they do the work).
- **Decides or informs:** which decisions they own (`D-…`), or whether they inform or are only affected. Every decision needs exactly one owner; if two roles claim it, that is a conflict (`X-`).
- **Position:** supports, opposes or unknown, with the source. Do not guess positions from job titles.
- **Status:** reached, not yet reached, or not reachable.

Then look for **missing voices**: the people affected who have not been heard, typically end users, the people who would operate or support the solution, and anyone who carries a risk (finance, legal, security). Name them in the brief. A discovery that has heard only managers about a tool for staff says so in its confidence ratings.

Record people by role. When two people share a role but differ (two sales reps with opposite views), give each a row and its own ID, with a distinguishing role label such as "Sales rep, north region".

## Questions by stakeholder

Questions follow `questions.md`. In Standard Discovery each question names its recipient (`P-…`), and up to ten may be open per round, so that one round reaches every stakeholder who matters.

## The question pack

When stakeholders answer asynchronously, prepare a pack the user can send:

1. Group the open questions by recipient.
2. For each recipient, draft a short message in plain language: one or two sentences of context (what is being decided and why their view matters), the questions in priority order, and a note that "don't know" and rough answers are useful.
3. Never include other stakeholders' statements in a message unless the user asks; quoting one person to another can damage trust and lead the answer.
4. The user sends the messages. Mark each question `asked` with the date when the user confirms it went out.

Show the pack in chat or, if the user wants it kept, as a section the user can delete later. Do not store contact details.

## Replies that arrive out of order

Replies come in pieces, late, and sometimes answer a question nobody asked. For each reply:

1. Add a source (`SRC-…`, role and date) and record each statement as `S-` (or `F-` if it can be verified).
2. Set the questions it answers to `answered (S-…)`. A partial answer keeps the question open with a note of what is still missing.
3. Follow the IDs that depend on it, as in the Skill's "Continue with new evidence". A reply can change a stakeholder's position or status; update the `P-` row.
4. If a reply contradicts an earlier one, record a conflict (`X-`) instead of letting the later reply win.
5. Tell the user, in a few lines, what the replies changed and which decisions or checkpoints they unblock.

A checkpoint does not have to wait for every reply. Proceed when the open questions left would not change the decision at that checkpoint, and say which ones you are proceeding without.
